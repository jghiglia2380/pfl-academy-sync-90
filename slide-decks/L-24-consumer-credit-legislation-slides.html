Key Terms

4 / 20

Annual Percentage Rate (APR)

The yearly cost of borrowing money, including both interest and fees expressed as a percentage

Example: A credit card with 18% APR means you'll pay $180 per year in interest on a $1,000 balance

Truth in Lending Act (TILA)

Federal law requiring lenders to clearly disclose all loan costs and terms before you sign

Example: TILA requires your mortgage lender to show you the total cost over the life of the loan

Key Terms

5 / 20

Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)

Governs credit bureaus and protects your right to dispute errors on your credit report

Example: FCRA entitles you to one free credit report per year from each bureau

Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA)

Prohibits lenders from discriminating based on race, gender, religion, or other protected classes

Example: ECOA prevents banks from denying a loan because of your marital status

Truth in Lending Act (1968)

6 / 20

Your Right to Know

TILA was the first major consumer credit law, requiring lenders to clearly disclose:

  • Annual Percentage Rate (APR)
  • Total finance charges
  • Amount financed
  • Total of all payments
  • Payment schedule

3-Day Rescission Right

For certain loans (like home equity), you can cancel within 3 business days

ECOA: Protected Classes in Lending

7 / 20

Protected by ECOA

  • Race or color
  • Religion
  • National origin
  • Sex (including gender identity)
  • Marital status
  • Age (if old enough to contract)
  • Receipt of public assistance

Lenders MAY Consider

  • Credit score and history
  • Income and employment
  • Debt-to-income ratio
  • Collateral value
  • Loan purpose
  • Down payment amount
  • Length of employment

Fair Credit Reporting Act (1970)

8 / 20

Protecting Your Credit Identity

FCRA ensures accuracy, fairness, and privacy of information in your credit files:

  • Free annual credit report from each bureau
  • Right to dispute inaccurate information
  • Credit bureaus must investigate disputes within 30 days
  • Limits who can access your credit report
  • Notification when information is used against you

Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (1977)

9 / 20

Protection from Abusive Debt Collectors

The FDCPA eliminates abusive practices by third-party debt collectors. It establishes clear rules for how, when, and where collectors can contact you.

Collectors CANNOT

Call before 8 AM or after 9 PM, use threats or profanity, claim to be attorneys (unless true), contact you at work if told not to

Collectors MUST

Identify themselves, provide written debt verification within 5 days, stop contacting you if you request in writing

Key Right: You can request debt validation. The collector must prove you owe the debt before continuing collection attempts.

Credit CARD Act (2009)

10 / 20

Credit Card Consumer Protections

The CARD Act established fair and transparent practices for credit card companies:

  • 45-day notice required before rate increases
  • Cannot raise rates on existing balances (with exceptions)
  • Payments applied to highest-rate balances first
  • Restricts fees and penalty charges
  • Protects young consumers under 21

Modern Enforcement: The CFPB

12 / 20

Consumer Financial Protection Bureau

Created in 2010 by the Dodd-Frank Act, the CFPB is the main federal agency enforcing consumer credit laws today.

  • Oversees banks, credit unions, and lenders
  • Enforces all major consumer credit laws
  • Accepts and investigates consumer complaints
  • Educates consumers about their rights
  • Restricts unfair, deceptive, and abusive practices

Real-World Scenario: Misleading Credit Offer

13 / 20

Javier's Situation

Javier receives a credit card offer advertising "0% interest!" in large letters. When the card arrives, he discovers the 0% only applies to the first 6 months, after which the rate jumps to 24.99% APR. The terms were buried in fine print.

The Problem

  • Misleading advertising made the offer seem better than it was
  • Important terms hidden in fine print
  • Javier made decisions based on incomplete information

Laws That Protect Him

  • TILA: Requires clear disclosure of all credit terms and APR
  • CARD Act: Mandates transparency in credit card advertising
  • Javier can file a complaint with the CFPB

Real-World Scenario: Harassing Debt Collector

14 / 20

Lena's Situation

Lena fell behind on a medical bill and it was sent to collections. The debt collector calls her at 11 PM, threatens to tell her employer, and calls her workplace repeatedly even after she asked them to stop.

FDCPA Violations

  • Calling after 9 PM (illegal)
  • Threatening to contact employer about debt
  • Continuing to call workplace after being told to stop
  • Harassment and intimidation tactics

Lena's Rights

  • Send written cease communication request
  • File complaint with FTC and CFPB
  • Sue for statutory damages up to $1,000 per violation
  • Collect attorney fees if she wins
