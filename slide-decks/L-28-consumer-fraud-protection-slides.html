Key Terms

4 / 20

Phishing

Fraudulent emails or messages designed to trick you into providing sensitive information

Example: An email pretending to be from Netflix asking you to "verify" your payment info

Spoofing

When scammers disguise their communication to appear as if it's from a legitimate source

Example: A call that shows your bank's name on caller ID but is actually a scammer

Key Terms

5 / 20

Identity Theft

Stealing personal information to open accounts, make purchases, or commit other fraud

Example: Someone uses your Social Security number to open credit cards in your name

Ponzi Scheme

A fraudulent investment that pays earlier investors with funds from newer investors

Example: Bernie Madoff's scheme that defrauded investors of $65 billion

Common Types of Consumer Fraud

Spoofing

Ponzi Schemes

"Investment" scams paying old investors with new investor money

Credit Repair Scams

Companies charging fees for "credit fixes" they can't deliver

Red Flags: Warning Signs of Fraud

7 / 20

How to Spot a Scam

  • Urgency: "Act NOW or your account will be closed!"
  • Too good to be true: Unrealistic returns or prizes
  • Request for personal info: Asking for passwords, SSN, etc.
  • Suspicious links: URLs that don't match the company
  • Poor grammar/spelling: Many scam messages have errors
  • Unusual payment methods: Wire transfers, gift cards, crypto

Fraud Prevention Strategies

8 / 20

1. Know Your Rights

  • Free annual credit report from each bureau
  • Right to place credit freezes
  • Report fraud to FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov

2. Secure Your Information

  • Use strong, unique passwords
  • Enable two-factor authentication
  • Limit what you share on social media

3. Verify Sources

  • Contact companies directly via official channels
  • Don't click links in unsolicited messages
  • Research investments thoroughly

4. Report Suspicious Activity

  • Report scams to the FTC
  • Alert your bank immediately
  • File police report for identity theft

Credit Freezes: Your Best Defense

10 / 20

Lock Down Your Credit

A credit freeze prevents new accounts from being opened in your name - even if a scammer has all your information.

  • Free to place and lift (since 2018)
  • Must freeze with all 3 bureaus: Equifax, Experian, TransUnion
  • Doesn't affect your credit score
  • You get a PIN to unfreeze when needed
  • Consider freezing proactively, not just after fraud

Scenario: Email Phishing Attack

13 / 20

Alex's Suspicious Email

Alex received an email claiming to be from his bank about "suspicious activity" on his account. The email urged him to "click here immediately" to verify his information. The sender's email was BankofAmerica1@secure-verify.net.

Red Flags in This Message

  • Urgency: "Click here IMMEDIATELY"
  • Suspicious email domain (not official bank)
  • Generic greeting (not personalized)
  • Request to verify sensitive information

What Alex Should Do

  • Do NOT click any links in the email
  • Call bank directly using number on his card
  • Report the phishing email to the bank
  • Delete the suspicious email

Scenario: Investment Catfishing

14 / 20

Miguel's "Investment Opportunity"

Miguel connected with someone on social media claiming to be a financial advisor. After building trust over several weeks, this person convinced Miguel to invest in a "guaranteed return" opportunity that required sending money via wire transfer.

Warning Signs Miguel Missed

  • "Guaranteed returns" - no investment is guaranteed
  • Met through social media, not verified channels
  • Wire transfer requested (untraceable)
  • Pressure to invest quickly

The Consequences

  • Money sent via wire transfer is nearly impossible to recover
  • "Advisor" disappears after receiving funds
  • Miguel has no legal recourse for recovery
  • Emotional impact from betrayed trust

Key Takeaways

Trust Your Gut
