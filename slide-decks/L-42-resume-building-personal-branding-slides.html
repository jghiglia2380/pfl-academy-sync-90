Essential Resume Components

7 / 18

1. Contact Information

  • Full name (slightly larger)
  • Phone number
  • Professional email
  • Location (city, state)
  • LinkedIn profile (optional)

2. Professional Summary

  • 2-4 sentence overview
  • Tailored to the position
  • Highlights key qualifications
  • Your unique value proposition

3. Education

  • Institution and location
  • Degree and major/minor
  • Graduation date
  • GPA (if 3.0+)
  • Relevant coursework

4. Work Experience

  • Company, location, dates
  • Job title
  • 3-5 bullet points
  • Action verbs first
  • Quantified results

5. Skills

  • Technical skills
  • Software proficiency
  • Language abilities
  • Certifications

6. Additional Sections

  • Volunteer experience
  • Leadership positions
  • Projects
  • Awards/honors

Resume Best Practices

10 / 18

📝 Keep It Concise

  • One page for entry-level
  • Use bullet points, not paragraphs
  • Include only relevant information
  • Quality over quantity

📐 Format Consistently

  • Same font style and size
  • Consistent spacing/alignment
  • Use bold/italics sparingly
  • Easy to scan quickly

🎯 Tailor Each Time

  • Research the job description
  • Highlight relevant skills
  • Mirror key terms
  • Adjust professional summary

Introduction to Personal Branding

11 / 18

Why Personal Branding Matters

Stand Out: In a competitive job market, differentiation is key

Build Recognition: Create memorability with employers and contacts

Establish Credibility: Build trust and authority in your field

Attract Opportunities: Draw positions aligned with your goals

💡 Your personal brand is how you present yourself professionally to the world—it's your reputation.

Key Elements of Your Brand:

  • Value Proposition: What unique combination do you offer?
  • Target Audience: Who needs to know about you?
  • Consistency: Same message across platforms
  • Authenticity: Genuine strengths and values

Building Your Personal Brand

12 / 18

Four Steps to Develop Your Brand

  • 1. Self-Assessment
    What are your core strengths? What makes you different? What are your professional values?
  • 2. Brand Statement
    Create a brief statement capturing your professional identity—like an elevator pitch
  • 3. Visual Identity
    Professional photo, consistent colors/fonts, clean professional email address
  • 4. Content Strategy
    Topics you'll discuss, expertise you'll demonstrate, tone and style

📌 Example Brand Statement: "I'm a business analytics student with strong data visualization skills and experience in retail market research. I combine technical expertise with creative problem-solving to help organizations translate data into actionable strategies."

Real-World Example

13 / 18
💻
Carlos: Resume Transformation

Carlos was applying for programming internships but wasn't getting interview calls despite having relevant coursework and projects.

Before: Listed coursework without practical applications, used generic descriptions like "Worked on database project," had a vague objective statement.

After: Added specific programming languages and tools, transformed descriptions: "Developed a database application that reduced data retrieval time by 40%", created a targeted professional summary.

Real-World Example

14 / 18
🎨
Maya: Building a Personal Brand

Maya wanted to establish herself in graphic design but was competing with many other design graduates.

Brand Positioning: Identified her niche as "sustainable design specialist who transforms complex information into clear visual narratives."

Cohesive Identity: Developed a personal logo, used consistent green/blue color palette across resume, portfolio, and social media. Even used recycled materials for printed portfolio.

Content Strategy: Shared case studies emphasizing environmental impact, posted design tips on sustainable practices.
