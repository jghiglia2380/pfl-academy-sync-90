Key Terms

4 / 20
W-2 Form

Document from your employer reporting wages and taxes withheld

Provided by January 31 each year; shows your earnings and what was already paid to IRS

Key boxes: Box 1 (wages), Box 2 (federal tax withheld)
1099 Forms

Documents reporting income from sources other than employment

Freelance work, investments, bank interest, etc.—no taxes are automatically withheld

Common types: 1099-NEC (freelance), 1099-INT (interest), 1099-DIV (dividends)

Key Terms

5 / 20
Form 1040

The main federal income tax return form for individuals

Report all income, claim deductions and credits, calculate what you owe (or are owed)

May include schedules: A (itemized), C (self-employment), D (capital gains)
Filing Threshold

Minimum income level at which you're required to file a tax return

Based on age, filing status, and income type—adjusted annually for inflation

2024: Single under 65 = $14,600; Self-employed $400+ always must file

Why File Even If Not Required?

10 / 20

Don't Leave Money on the Table!

  • Get your withheld taxes back: If employer withheld taxes but you owe nothing, only way to get it back is to file
  • Claim refundable credits: Earned Income Tax Credit can give you a refund even if you owe $0
  • Prevent identity theft: File first so no one can fraudulently use your SSN
  • Start the clock: IRS has 3 years from filing to audit
  • Build your record: Tax returns verify income for loans, FAFSA, apartments

Self-Employment: Special Rules

12 / 20

Freelance? Side Hustle? Gig Work?

Self-employment has different tax requirements:

  • $400+ = must file regardless of other income thresholds
  • Self-employment tax: ~15.3% for Social Security + Medicare
  • No withholding: You must save and pay taxes yourself
  • Quarterly payments: May need to pay estimated taxes 4x/year
  • Schedule C: Report income and deduct business expenses

Real-World Example

13 / 20
A

Ava: Filing for a Refund

High school student, part-time job at coffee shop

Ava earned $4,500 last year—well below the $14,600 filing threshold. Her employer withheld $380 in federal taxes from her paychecks throughout the year.

Even though she's NOT required to file, she SHOULD file. Why? At her income level, she owes $0 in taxes. Filing lets her get all $380 back! If she doesn't file within 3 years, that money becomes property of the U.S. Treasury.

Real-World Example

14 / 20
B

Ben: Multiple Income Sources

Full-time retail job + freelance graphic design

Ben earned $32,000 from his retail job (W-2) plus $5,000 from freelance design (1099-NEC). He must file for two reasons: total income exceeds threshold AND self-employment income exceeds $400.

Ben needs Form 1040, Schedule C (business income/expenses), and Schedule SE (self-employment tax). He'll owe ~$765 in self-employment tax on his freelance income, but can deduct business expenses like software and equipment.

Key Takeaways

📋

Filing requirements depend on income, age, and filing status—but self-employment income of $400+ always requires filing

💰

File even if not required to get refunds of withheld taxes, claim credits, and prevent identity theft
