Key Terms

4 / 20
Tax Deduction
Definition

An amount that reduces your taxable income before taxes are calculated, lowering the base on which your tax is computed.

Examples

Mortgage interest, charitable donations, student loan interest, medical expenses

Tax Credit
Definition

An amount that directly reduces your tax bill dollar-for-dollar after taxes are calculated.

Examples

Child Tax Credit, Earned Income Tax Credit, American Opportunity Credit

Key Terms

5 / 20
Standard Deduction
Definition

A fixed amount based on filing status that reduces taxable income—no itemizing needed. Used by ~90% of filers.

2023 Amounts

Single: $13,850 • Married Filing Jointly: $27,700 • Head of Household: $20,800

Refundable vs. Nonrefundable
Definition

Refundable credits can result in a refund if they exceed your tax bill. Nonrefundable credits can only reduce your tax to zero.

Key Difference

EITC (refundable) → can get $ back • Child Care Credit (nonrefundable) → only reduces to $0

How Tax Deductions Work

6 / 20

Deductions Reduce Taxable Income

Tax deductions work by lowering the amount of income subject to taxation. This happens BEFORE your tax is calculated.

The actual tax savings depends on your marginal tax rate (tax bracket). Higher bracket = more valuable deductions.

💡 A $10,000 deduction in the 22% bracket saves $2,200. The same deduction in the 12% bracket saves only $1,200.

Tax Deductions vs. Tax Credits

7 / 20

📋 Deductions

  • Reduce taxable INCOME
  • Applied BEFORE tax calculated
  • Value varies by tax bracket
  • Higher brackets benefit more
  • Must choose: standard or itemized
  • $1,000 = $220 savings (22% bracket)
VS

💵 Credits

  • Reduce TAX BILL directly
  • Applied AFTER tax calculated
  • Dollar-for-dollar savings
  • Equal benefit all brackets
  • May be refundable or not
  • $1,000 = $1,000 savings always

The Power of Tax Credits

8 / 20

Credits: Dollar-for-Dollar Savings

A tax credit directly reduces the amount of tax you owe. If you owe $5,000 in taxes and have a $1,000 credit, you now owe $4,000.

This makes credits particularly powerful—a $1,000 credit saves exactly $1,000, regardless of your tax bracket.

💰 A relatively small tax credit can be MORE valuable than a larger tax deduction, especially for lower-income taxpayers.

Common Tax Deductions

9 / 20

Standard vs. Itemized Deductions

You must choose one: the standard deduction (a fixed amount) OR itemized deductions (sum of specific expenses). Choose whichever is larger.

  • Mortgage Interest: Interest on home loans up to $750,000
  • State & Local Taxes (SALT): Up to $10,000 in state income, property, and sales taxes
  • Charitable Donations: Gifts to qualified organizations
  • Medical Expenses: Amounts exceeding 7.5% of your income
  • Student Loan Interest: Up to $2,500 (even with standard deduction!)
  • Educator Expenses: Teachers can deduct up to $300 for supplies

Why This Matters for Your Future

11 / 20

Tax Knowledge = Financial Power

Understanding deductions and credits can save you thousands of dollars throughout your life. This knowledge helps you make smarter decisions about:

  • Education Decisions: Timing tuition payments to maximize education credits
  • Housing Choices: Understanding when mortgage interest deduction makes renting vs. buying decisions clearer
  • Retirement Saving: Contributing to tax-advantaged accounts for current deductions AND future savings
  • Charitable Giving: Strategic timing of donations to maximize deduction benefits
  • Career Planning: Self-employment deductions, work-related expenses
  • Healthcare: HSA contributions provide triple tax advantages

Strategic Tax Planning

12 / 20

Smart Strategies to Reduce Taxes

Timing matters. Sometimes delaying income or accelerating deductions can result in significant tax savings.

"Bunching" deductions: If your itemized deductions are close to the standard deduction, consider making two years' worth of charitable donations in one year to exceed the threshold.

🎯 Tax planning isn't about cheating—it's about using legal strategies to keep more of what you earn.
