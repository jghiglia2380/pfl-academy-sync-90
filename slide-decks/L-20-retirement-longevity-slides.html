The Impact of Living Longer

6 / 20

Retirement Is Getting Longer

Life expectancy has increased dramatically over the past century. Today, a 65-year-old can expect to live into their mid-80s, with many living well into their 90s or beyond.

This extended lifespan means retirement savings may need to last 20, 30, or even 40 years—far longer than previous generations planned for.

💡 The traditional 10-15 year retirement has evolved into potentially 20-30+ years, requiring substantially more savings.

Time Is Your Greatest Asset

8 / 20

Compounding: The Snowball Effect

Compounding occurs when your investment returns generate their own returns, creating exponential growth over time.

Morgan invested only $18,000 but ended up with nearly as much as Jordan who invested $70,000. Why? Because Morgan's money had more time to compound.

🎯 Starting 9 years earlier with less money nearly matched investing 4x more but starting later. Time beats money!

Inflation: The Silent Retirement Threat

9 / 20

Your Money Loses Value Over Time

Inflation represents one of the most significant challenges to retirement planning. Even modest inflation rates can substantially reduce purchasing power over an extended retirement period.

  • At 3% inflation: $100 today = $55 purchasing power in 20 years
  • In 20 years: What costs $100 today will cost about $181
  • In 30 years: What costs $100 today will cost about $243
  • Rule of 72: Prices double approximately every 24 years at 3% inflation

💰 A 25-year retirement means you'll need nearly twice as much income at age 90 as at age 65 just to maintain the same lifestyle!

2 Extend working years (even part-time)

Why This Matters Now

11 / 20

The Decisions You Make Today Shape Your Tomorrow

Understanding longevity and retirement planning while you're young gives you the greatest advantage possible: time. The earlier you start, the easier it is to build wealth.

  • 40+ years of compounding: Starting at 22 vs 32 can double your retirement savings
  • Habit formation: Establishing saving habits early makes them automatic
  • Flexibility: More time allows you to recover from market downturns
  • Lower required savings: Starting early means smaller monthly contributions
  • Employer match: Never pass up free money from 401(k) matching
  • Tax advantages: Roth IRAs provide tax-free growth over decades
