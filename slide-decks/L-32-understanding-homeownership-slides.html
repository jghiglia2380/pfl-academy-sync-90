Key Terms

4 / 20
Mortgage
Definition

A loan specifically for purchasing real estate, where the property itself serves as collateral for the lender.

Common Terms

15-year or 30-year repayment periods; fixed or adjustable interest rates

Down Payment
Definition

Initial upfront payment toward the purchase price, typically 3-20% of the home's value.

Example

A $300K home with 10% down = $30,000 down payment, $270K mortgage

Key Terms

5 / 20
PMI
Definition

Private Mortgage Insurance—required when down payment is less than 20%, protecting the lender if you default.

Cost Impact

Adds 0.5-1% of loan annually; can be removed once you reach 20% equity

Home Equity
Definition

The difference between your home's market value and what you still owe—your ownership stake.

Example

Home worth $350K, mortgage balance $250K = $100K equity (your wealth)

The Home Buying Process

6 / 20

6 Steps from Preparation to Keys in Hand

Purchasing a home typically takes 2-3 months from offer to closing—but preparation starts 6-12 months earlier.

  • 1. Financial Prep (6-12 mo): Improve credit, save for down payment, reduce debt
  • 2. Get Pre-Approved: Submit documents to lenders, know your budget
  • 3. House Hunting: Work with agent, tour homes, research neighborhoods
  • 4. Make an Offer: Submit offer with contingencies, negotiate terms
  • 5. Inspection & Appraisal: Verify condition and value, negotiate repairs
  • 6. Closing: Sign documents, pay closing costs, get keys!

Types of Mortgages

7 / 20

Finding the Right Loan for You

Different mortgage programs exist to accommodate various financial situations. Your options depend on credit score, down payment savings, and eligibility.

Conventional Loans: Most common, 3-20% down, 620+ credit score, PMI if under 20% down.

FHA Loans: Government-backed, as low as 3.5% down, 580+ credit, ideal for first-time buyers.

🏠 Special programs exist for veterans (VA loans) and rural buyers (USDA loans)—both offer 0% down options.

Understanding PITI Payments

Your total monthly mortgage payment includes four components—PITI:

P Principal (pays down loan balance)
I Interest (cost of borrowing)
T Taxes (property taxes, held in escrow)
I Insurance (homeowner's + PMI if applicable)
30 yr
Most Common Loan Term

Fixed vs. Adjustable:

  • Fixed: Same rate forever, predictable payments
  • ARM: Lower initial rate, then adjusts
  • 5/1 ARM = fixed for 5 years, then adjusts annually
  • ARMs carry risk if rates rise significantly

True Costs: Ongoing Expenses

10 / 20

Monthly and Annual Costs Beyond the Mortgage

Your mortgage payment is just one piece. Budget for all these ongoing expenses to understand your true housing cost.

  • Property Taxes: 0.5-2.5% of home value annually (varies by location)
  • Homeowner's Insurance: $800-$1,500+ annually for average homes
  • PMI (if applicable): 0.5-1% of loan amount annually
  • HOA Fees: $100-$700+ monthly in applicable communities
  • Maintenance: Budget ~1% of home value annually ($2,500 for $250K home)
  • Utilities: All utilities now your responsibility—budget $200-$400+/month

💡 A common mistake: only budgeting for the mortgage. Total housing costs are typically 30-50% higher.

Are You Ready to Buy?

12 / 20

Financial & Lifestyle Readiness

Homeownership isn't right for everyone at every stage of life. Honestly assess your readiness across multiple dimensions:

Financial: Stable income, adequate savings, low debt, good credit score (620+ conventional, 580+ FHA).

Lifestyle: Plan to stay 3-5+ years, willing to handle maintenance, time for property upkeep.

Market: Interest rates, local housing prices, rent-vs-buy math in your area.

📊 Use the 28/36 rule: Housing costs under 28% of income, total debt under 36%.

Common Misconceptions

14 / 20

Myths About Buying a Home

Don't let common misconceptions drive your housing decisions. Get the facts before committing to one of life's biggest financial moves.

  • "You need 20% down": Many programs allow 3-3.5% down (though you'll pay PMI)
  • "Buying is always better than renting": Depends on how long you stay, market conditions, opportunity cost
  • "Real estate always goes up": Values can and do decline—see 2008
  • "Mortgage payment = housing cost": True cost includes taxes, insurance, maintenance, repairs
  • "Buy the most expensive home you qualify for": Being house-poor leaves no room for other goals
  • "Renting is throwing money away": Renting provides flexibility and frees capital for other investments
