Key Terms

4 / 20

Premium

The amount you pay to maintain insurance coverage, typically monthly, quarterly, or annually
Example: In {{STATE_NAME}}, teen drivers pay ~${{STATE_INSURANCE_AVG_TEEN}}/month for auto insurance

Deductible

The amount you must pay out-of-pocket before your insurance coverage begins
Example: With a $500 deductible, you pay the first $500 of a claim

Coverage Limit

The maximum amount an insurance policy will pay for a covered loss
Example: Auto liability of $100,000/$300,000 means max $100K per person, $300K per accident

Exclusion

Specific conditions or circumstances not covered by an insurance policy
Example: Pre-existing conditions, intentional damage, or acts of war

Key Terms

5 / 20

Claim

A formal request to an insurance company for coverage or compensation for a covered loss
Example: Filing a claim after your car is damaged in a hailstorm

Co-insurance

The percentage of costs you pay after meeting your deductible
Example: 80/20 co-insurance means insurance pays 80%, you pay 20%

Beneficiary

The person or entity designated to receive benefits from an insurance policy
Example: Naming your spouse as beneficiary on your life insurance policy

Rider

An optional addition to an insurance policy that provides additional coverage
Example: Adding flood coverage rider to your homeowner's policy

How Insurance Works

6 / 20

Risk Transfer Through Pooling

Insurance is a risk transfer mechanism where you pay a small, predictable amount (premium) to protect against a larger, unpredictable loss.

The Insurance Process:

  • Premium Payment: Pay regular amounts for coverage
  • Risk Assessment: Company evaluates your risk level
  • Coverage Agreement: Policy outlines what's covered
  • Claims Process: File a claim when loss occurs
  • Benefit Payment: Receive payment per policy terms
👥

Risk Pooling

Many contribute, few collect when needed

💵

Predictable Costs

Small premiums vs. large unexpected losses

📋

Contract Protection

Written agreement defines all terms

The Premium-Deductible Relationship

7 / 20

💰 Lower Premiums

Ways to reduce your monthly costs:

  • Choose higher deductibles
  • Bundle multiple policies
  • Maintain good credit score
  • Take advantage of discounts
  • Shop around and compare

📊 Deductible Trade-off

Higher deductible = lower premium

  • $250 deductible → Higher monthly premium
  • $500 deductible → Medium premium
  • $1,000 deductible → Lower premium
  • $2,500 deductible → Lowest premium
  • Must be able to afford the deductible!

🎯 Coverage Limits

Maximum the insurer will pay:

  • Per-incident limits
  • Aggregate (annual) limits
  • Per-person limits (liability)
  • Higher limits = higher premiums
  • Match to your assets/risks

⚠️ Exclusions

What's NOT covered:

  • Pre-existing conditions (health)
  • Intentional damage
  • High-risk activities
  • Acts of war/terrorism
  • Always read the fine print!

Life Insurance: Protecting Your Family

10 / 20

Term Life

Coverage for a specific period (10, 20, 30 years)

  • Lowest cost option
  • Pure death benefit protection
  • No cash value component
  • Ideal for temporary needs
  • Good for young families

Whole Life

Lifetime coverage with cash value

  • Coverage never expires
  • Builds cash value over time
  • Higher premiums than term
  • Can borrow against policy
  • Premiums stay level

Universal Life

Flexible premiums and death benefits

  • Adjustable coverage amounts
  • Flexible premium payments
  • Cash value component
  • More complex than term
  • Interest rate sensitive

Real-World Example: Sarah's Surgery

13 / 20

Health Insurance in Action

Sarah, 28, has employer health insurance with a $2,000 deductible and 80/20 co-insurance. She needs an appendectomy costing $20,000.

How her insurance works:

  • • Sarah pays first $2,000 (deductible)
  • • Remaining $18,000 split 80/20
  • • Sarah's 20% = $3,600
  • • Insurance's 80% = $14,400

✓ With Insurance

Sarah pays $5,600 total ($2,000 + $3,600)

✗ Without Insurance

Sarah would owe the full $20,000

💡 Savings

Insurance saved Sarah $14,400 on this single procedure

Real-World Examples: Auto & Life Insurance

14 / 20

🚗 Marcus's Hailstorm

Comprehensive auto with $500 deductible

  • Hail damages car: $3,200 in repairs
  • Marcus pays: $500 (deductible)
  • Insurance pays: $2,700
  • Without insurance: $3,200 out-of-pocket

💼 James's Life Insurance

30-year term, $500K death benefit

  • Two children, mortgage, spouse Melissa
  • If James passes, Melissa receives $500K
  • Tax-free benefit for beneficiary
  • Can pay off mortgage, fund education

🎯 Key Insight

Each insurance type serves a specific purpose: Health protects against medical costs, Auto covers vehicle damage and liability, and Life provides financial security for dependents. The right combination depends on your unique situation, assets, and responsibilities.
