Key Terms

4 / 20
Principal
Definition

The original amount of money borrowed, before any interest or fees are added.

Example

You borrow $20,000 for a car—$20,000 is your principal amount

APR
Definition

Annual Percentage Rate—the yearly cost of borrowing, including interest AND fees.

Why It Matters

APR lets you compare true costs; a 6% rate with fees may have a 7% APR

Key Terms

5 / 20
Secured Credit
Definition

Credit backed by collateral that the lender can claim if you don't repay.

Examples

Auto loans, mortgages—the car or house is the collateral

Unsecured Credit
Definition

Credit not backed by collateral; lender relies on your promise to repay.

Examples

Credit cards, personal loans, student loans—higher interest rates

Secured vs. Unsecured Credit

6 / 20

Collateral Affects Everything

Secured credit requires you to pledge an asset. If you fail to pay, the lender can take the asset—this reduces their risk.

Unsecured credit has no collateral backing it. The lender has only your promise to repay, increasing their risk.

💡 Lower lender risk = lower interest rates for YOU. Secured loans almost always have lower rates than unsecured loans.

Secured Loan Examples:

  • Mortgage (house is collateral)
  • Auto loan (car is collateral)
  • Secured credit card (cash deposit)

Installment vs. Non-Installment Credit

7 / 20

📅 Installment Credit

  • Fixed payment amount each month
  • Set number of payments (e.g., 60 months)
  • You know exactly when it's paid off
  • Easier to budget and plan
  • Examples: auto loans, mortgages
VS

🔄 Non-Installment Credit

  • Variable payments (often just minimum)
  • Revolving: pay down, borrow again
  • No set payoff date
  • Can lead to perpetual debt
  • Examples: credit cards, lines of credit

When Borrowing Makes Sense

9 / 20

Strategic Borrowing Can Build Wealth

Not all debt is bad. Borrowing can be a strategic financial decision when it ultimately improves your financial position:

  • Education: Student loans may increase earning potential by $500K+ over your lifetime
  • Housing: Mortgages build equity; you own an asset instead of paying rent forever
  • Transportation: Auto loans provide reliable transportation for work
  • Business: Business loans fund ventures that generate income
  • Emergencies: Some urgent situations (medical, essential repairs) require immediate funds
  • Appreciating assets: Borrowing for things that gain value over time

🎯 Ask yourself: "Will this loan help me earn more, save more, or build wealth over time?"

When to Avoid Borrowing

10 / 20

Red Flags for Borrowing

Borrowing should generally be avoided in these situations:

❌ Luxuries and wants (vacations, entertainment)
❌ Regular expenses (groceries, utilities)
❌ Impulse purchases you haven't planned for
❌ When already financially strained

Why Understanding Borrowing Matters

11 / 20

Your Borrowing Decisions Shape Your Future

The decisions you make about borrowing will affect your financial life for years or even decades. Understanding the true cost gives you power:

  • Avoid costly mistakes: A bad car loan can cost you thousands more than necessary
  • Negotiate better terms: Knowledge helps you get lower rates and better deals
  • Build credit wisely: Strategic borrowing and repayment builds your credit score
  • Achieve goals faster: Smart borrowing for education or housing accelerates wealth-building
  • Avoid debt traps: Recognize predatory lending and high-cost options like payday loans
  • Maintain financial freedom: Keep debt manageable so you have options in life

Key Factors That Affect Loan Costs

12 / 20

What Determines Your True Cost?

Several factors combine to determine how much you'll actually pay when you borrow money:

Interest Rate: The percentage the lender charges for borrowing money.

Loan Term: Longer terms = lower payments but MORE total interest.

Payment Strategy: Paying more than minimum saves thousands.

💰 On a $20,000 loan, choosing 3 years vs. 6 years could save you $2,000+ in interest.

Your Credit Score Matters:

  • Excellent (750+): Best rates available
  • Good (700-749): Favorable rates
  • Fair (650-699): Higher rates
  • Poor (below 650): Highest rates or denial

Ways to Lower Your Cost:

  • Improve credit score before borrowing
  • Choose shorter loan terms
  • Make larger down payments
  • Pay more than the minimum
