Key Terms

4 / 20
Budget

A plan that tracks income and expenses to guide financial decisions

Budgets provide financial freedom by ensuring your money goes where you want it to go

Example: Tracking monthly income of $3,000 across categories
50/30/20 Rule

A budgeting framework for allocating income

50% to needs, 30% to wants, and 20% to savings and debt repayment

Example: On $3,000/month: $1,500 needs, $900 wants, $600 savings

Key Terms

5 / 20
Emergency Fund

Money set aside specifically for unexpected expenses

Provides a buffer between you and life's uncertainties—job loss, car repairs, medical bills

Goal: 3-6 months of essential expenses; start with $500-1,000
Compound Interest

Earning interest on both principal and accumulated interest

Creates exponential growth over long periods—your money makes money

Example: $1,000 at 7% becomes $2,000 in ~10 years, $4,000 in ~20 years

Real-World Example

13 / 20
C

Casey's Proactive Approach

Following the 50/30/20 rule with $3,000/month

Casey creates a monthly budget: $1,500 (50%) for needs including rent, utilities, groceries, and transportation. $900 (30%) for wants like dining out, entertainment, and hobbies. $600 (20%) for savings—$300 to emergency fund, $200 to retirement, $100 extra to student loans.

Casey tracks all expenses weekly using a budgeting app, automates savings transfers, and has built a 3-month emergency fund ($4,500) over the past year.
