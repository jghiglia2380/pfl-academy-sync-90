Key Terms

4 / 20

Federal Student Loans

Loans issued by the U.S. Department of Education with standardized terms and borrower protections

Example: Direct Subsidized Loans don't accrue interest while you're in school

Private Student Loans

Education loans from banks, credit unions, or other private lenders with varying terms

Example: A loan from a bank that requires a credit check and co-signer

Key Terms

5 / 20

Income-Driven Repayment

Federal loan payment plans that cap monthly payments based on your income and family size

Example: SAVE plan limits payments to 5-10% of discretionary income

Loan Forgiveness

Programs that cancel remaining loan balances after meeting specific criteria

Example: Public Service Loan Forgiveness after 10 years of qualifying payments

Federal vs. Private Loans

7 / 20

Federal Loans

  • Fixed interest rates
  • No credit check required
  • Income-driven repayment plans
  • Forgiveness programs available
  • Deferment during hardship
  • One application (FAFSA)

Private Loans

  • Variable or fixed rates based on credit
  • Credit check required
  • Limited repayment flexibility
  • No forgiveness programs
  • Limited hardship options
  • Separate applications per lender

Federal Loan Repayment Options

9 / 20

Flexibility When You Need It

Federal loans offer multiple repayment paths designed to fit your financial situation after graduation.

Standard Repayment

Fixed payments over 10 years. Highest monthly payment but lowest total cost.

Income-Driven Plans

Payments based on income (5-20%). Remaining balance forgiven after 20-25 years.

Graduated Repayment

Payments start low and increase every 2 years. Good if you expect salary growth.

Extended Repayment

Lower monthly payments over 25 years. Higher total interest paid.

Case Study: Alex's Smart Strategy

13 / 20

Alex's Approach

Alex borrowed $20,000 in federal loans at 5% fixed interest. Works 10 hours/week during school to minimize borrowing and qualifies for income-driven repayment after graduation.

Key Advantages

  • Fixed rate locks in predictable payments
  • Access to income-driven plans if needed
  • Eligible for PSLF if entering public service
  • Work income reduces total borrowing

Financial Outcome

  • Total repayment: ~$25,500 over 10 years
  • Monthly payment: ~$212
  • Manageable debt-to-income ratio
  • Financial flexibility preserved

Case Study: Jordan's Mixed Approach

14 / 20

Jordan's Situation

Jordan borrowed $15,000 in federal loans AND $10,000 in private loans at 10% variable interest. Required a parent co-signer and has fixed repayment regardless of post-graduation income.

Challenges Faced

  • Variable rate could increase over time
  • No income-driven options for private loans
  • Parent's credit at risk as co-signer
  • Less flexibility during financial hardship

Financial Reality

  • Higher total interest paid
  • Less predictable monthly costs
  • No forgiveness options for private portion
  • More stress if income fluctuates
