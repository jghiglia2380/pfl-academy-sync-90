Key Terms

4 / 20

E-commerce

The buying and selling of goods and services over the internet

Example: Purchasing shoes from Amazon or booking travel on Expedia

Digital Wallet

An electronic service that stores payment information for secure online transactions

Example: Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal, or Venmo

Key Terms

5 / 20

Phishing

A fraudulent attempt to obtain sensitive information by disguising as a trustworthy entity

Example: A fake email pretending to be from your bank asking for login credentials

Encryption

The process of converting information into code to prevent unauthorized access

Example: HTTPS and the padlock icon mean your data is encrypted during transmission

Signs of a Secure Website

8 / 20

What to Look For

Before entering any payment information, verify you're on a legitimate, secure site.

HTTPS & Padlock

Look for "https://" and a padlock icon in the address bar - these indicate encryption

Contact Information

Legitimate retailers have a physical address, phone number, and customer service

Clear Policies

Return policies, privacy policies, and terms of service should be easy to find

Protecting Yourself Online

9 / 20

Before You Buy

  • Research the retailer's reputation
  • Check for HTTPS and padlock
  • Avoid public Wi-Fi for purchases
  • Use strong, unique passwords
  • Beware "too good to be true" prices

During Purchase

  • Use credit cards (better protection)
  • Consider digital wallets
  • Never wire money or send cash
  • Only provide necessary info
  • Save confirmation emails

After Purchase

  • Track your packages
  • Review bank statements
  • Report unauthorized charges
  • Know the return policy
  • Keep records of transactions

Choosing Secure Payment Methods

10 / 20

Not All Payment Methods Are Equal

Credit Cards (Best Protection)

Federal law limits your liability to $50 for unauthorized charges; most cards offer $0 liability

Digital Wallets (Good Protection)

Add layer of security - merchants never see your actual card number

Debit Cards (Less Protection)

Money comes directly from your bank account; harder to recover stolen funds

Warning Story: Mike's Costly Mistake

14 / 20

Mike's Situation

Mike found a popular gaming console on an unfamiliar website for 40% below retail. Excited by the deal, he made the purchase without researching the site. After payment, he only received an automated email.

Red Flags He Missed

  • Price 40% below retail (too good to be true)
  • Unfamiliar website with no reviews
  • Didn't research the retailer
  • No verification of site legitimacy

The Consequences

  • Console never arrived
  • Website disappeared completely
  • Had to dispute charge with credit card
  • Wasted time and caused stress
Discussion

What warning signs should have alerted Mike that the deal was a scam?

Think about price, website reputation, and verification steps. What would you have done differently in his situation?
Personal Reflection

For what types of products would you prefer shopping online vs. in-store?

Consider factors like needing to try items on, urgency, price sensitivity, and how comfortable you are with returns. How do your shopping preferences balance convenience with security?
