Standard 5: Saving & Investing
Essential Question

Should you save your money or invest it?

The answer isn't always straightforward — it depends on your goals, timeline, and risk tolerance. Understanding when to save versus invest is one of the most important financial decisions you'll make.

Key Terms

4 / 20
Saving
Definition

Setting aside money in safe, accessible accounts like savings accounts for short-term goals and emergencies

Example

Putting $200/month into a savings account for an emergency fund or vacation

Investing
Definition

Placing money in assets like stocks, bonds, or real estate that are expected to grow in value over time

Example

Buying shares in a diversified index fund for retirement 30 years away

Key Terms

5 / 20
Compounding
Definition

The process of earning returns on both your initial investment and previously earned returns — "interest on interest"

Example

$1,000 at 10% becomes $1,100 year 1, then $1,210 year 2 (earning interest on the $1,100)

Risk Tolerance
Definition

Your comfort level with the possibility of losing money in exchange for potentially higher returns

Example

Someone with high risk tolerance might invest heavily in stocks; low risk tolerance prefers bonds

Saving vs. Investing

6 / 20

Two Paths to Financial Growth

Both saving and investing help you build wealth, but they serve different purposes and work differently:

  • Saving — Prioritizes safety and access to your money
  • Investing — Prioritizes growth potential over time
  • Your choice depends on when you need the money
  • Most people need both strategies working together

Comparing Saving & Investing

7 / 20

💰 Saving

  • Purpose: Short-term goals, emergencies
  • Time Horizon: Under 5 years
  • Risk Level: Very low (FDIC insured)
  • Returns: Low but guaranteed
  • Liquidity: High — easy access anytime
  • Best For: Emergency funds, near-term purchases

📈 Investing

  • Purpose: Long-term wealth building
  • Time Horizon: 5+ years
  • Risk Level: Varies from low to high
  • Returns: Higher potential, not guaranteed
  • Liquidity: Lower — may fluctuate in value
  • Best For: Retirement, college, major goals

The Power of Compounding

8 / 20

Your Money Working for You

Compounding is often called the "eighth wonder of the world" because of its powerful effect over time:

  • You earn returns on your original investment
  • Then you earn returns on those returns
  • The longer you invest, the more powerful the effect
  • Starting early is more important than starting big
  • Time is your greatest advantage as a young investor

SMART Financial Goals

9 / 20

Setting Goals That Actually Work

The SMART framework transforms vague wishes into achievable financial goals. Each element makes your goal more actionable:

S - Specific

"Save $500 for a new laptop" is better than "save money." Know exactly what you want.

M - Measurable

Track your progress with numbers: "Put $50/month into savings" lets you see results.

A - Achievable

Set realistic targets based on your actual income and expenses. Challenge yourself, but be practical.

R - Relevant

Your goal should connect to what matters to you personally and align with your values.

T - Time-bound

Set a deadline: "Save $500 by December" creates urgency and accountability.

Risk and Opportunity Cost

10 / 20

Every Choice Has Trade-offs

Understanding risk and opportunity cost helps you make better financial decisions:

  • Risk — The possibility of losing some or all of your money
  • Reward — Higher risk investments typically offer higher potential returns
  • Opportunity Cost — What you give up by choosing one option over another
  • Keeping all money in savings = safe but loses to inflation over time
  • Diversification reduces risk without eliminating growth potential

Why This Matters for You

11 / 20

Your Biggest Advantage: Time

As a young person, you have something money can't buy — time. Understanding saving and investing now can dramatically change your financial future.

Build Security

An emergency fund (saving) protects you from unexpected expenses and gives you peace of mind and financial stability.

Create Wealth

Starting to invest in your teens or twenties can turn small amounts into significant wealth through decades of compounding.

Achieve Goals

From buying a car to retiring comfortably, every goal requires choosing the right strategy for your timeline.
