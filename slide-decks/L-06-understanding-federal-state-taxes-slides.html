Key Terms

4 / 20
Progressive Tax
Definition

A tax system where higher-income earners pay a higher percentage of their income in taxes.

Example

Federal income tax: 10% on first $11K, 12% on next $33K, 22% on amounts above that.

Tax Bracket
Definition

An income range that is subject to a specific tax rate in a progressive tax system.

Key Insight

Only income WITHIN each bracket is taxed at that rate—not your entire income!

Key Terms

5 / 20
FICA / Payroll Tax
Definition

Employment taxes funding Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%) — totaling 7.65%.

How It Works

Your employer matches your contribution, so 15.3% total goes to these programs.

Withholding
Definition

The portion of your paycheck your employer keeps to pay taxes on your behalf throughout the year.

Why It Matters

Prevents a large tax bill in April—taxes are paid as you earn income.

What Your Taxes Fund

6 / 20

Taxes Pay for Public Services We All Use

When you pay taxes, you're contributing to a wide range of programs and infrastructure that benefit society:

  • Education: Public schools, colleges, student aid programs
  • Transportation: Highways, bridges, public transit systems
  • Healthcare: Medicare, Medicaid, public health initiatives
  • Public Safety: Police, fire departments, emergency services
  • National Defense: Military and homeland security
  • Social Programs: Unemployment benefits, disability assistance
  • Environment: Parks, wildlife protection, EPA programs
  • Research: NASA, NIH, scientific advancement

💡 Understanding where tax money goes helps you participate in policy discussions about how it should be spent.

How Progressive Tax Brackets Work

7 / 20

The Common Misconception

Many people think: "If I'm in the 22% bracket, I pay 22% on ALL my income."

This is wrong! You only pay each rate on income within that specific bracket.

Example: $50,000 income

• First $11,000 → 10% = $1,100

• Next $33,725 → 12% = $4,047

• Last $5,275 → 22% = $1,161

📊 Total tax: $6,308 = 12.6% effective rate (not 22%!)

Payroll Taxes: FICA Explained

11 / 20

What FICA Funds

Social Security (6.2%): Retirement benefits, disability income, survivor benefits for families.

Medicare (1.45%): Healthcare coverage for those 65+ and certain disabled individuals.

Your employer matches your contribution, meaning 15.3% total goes to these programs for every dollar you earn.

🔄 Unlike income tax, FICA is a flat rate on all wages (SS has a cap at ~$168,600 in 2024).

Why This Matters for Your Future

14 / 20

Tax Knowledge = Better Financial Decisions

Understanding how taxes work helps you in multiple areas of life:

  • Job Offers: Compare after-tax income, not just salary numbers
  • Budgeting: Know your actual take-home pay before committing to expenses
  • Location: Consider total tax burden when choosing where to live
  • Retirement: Pre-tax contributions (401k) reduce your taxable income now
  • Side Income: Self-employment means paying both halves of FICA (15.3%)
  • Investments: Different investment income has different tax treatment

🎯 The financial decisions you make now—informed by tax knowledge—compound over your career.

Progressive vs. Regressive Taxes

16 / 20

Progressive Taxes

  • Higher earners pay higher %
  • Federal income tax
  • Most state income taxes
  • Based on "ability to pay"
  • Reduces income inequality
  • More complex to calculate
VS

Regressive Taxes

  • Everyone pays same rate
  • Sales tax, gas tax, fees
  • Lower earners pay higher % of income
  • Simple to administer
  • Can burden low-income families
  • Unavoidable on essentials

Key Takeaways

1

Progressive System

Federal tax rates increase with income, but only income in each bracket is taxed at that rate

2

States Vary Widely

From 0% to 13%+ income tax—but no-tax states often have higher property/sales taxes

3

FICA is Flat

Everyone pays 7.65% for Social Security and Medicare, regardless of income level

4

Consider Total Picture

When comparing locations, look at ALL taxes: income, property, sales, and fees
