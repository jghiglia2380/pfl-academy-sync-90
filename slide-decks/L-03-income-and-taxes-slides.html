Key Terms

4 / 20
Gross Income
Definition

The total amount of money earned before any deductions are taken out—your "on paper" salary.

Example

You earn $4,000/month salary. This is your gross income before taxes and deductions.

Net Income
Definition

The amount you actually receive after all deductions—your "take-home pay" that hits your bank account.

Example

After taxes and deductions, you receive $3,100/month. This is your net income.

Key Terms

5 / 20
FICA Taxes
Definition

Federal Insurance Contributions Act taxes that fund Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%).

Total Rate

7.65% of your gross income goes to FICA—you'll benefit from these programs in retirement.

W-4 Form
Definition

A tax form you complete when starting a job that tells your employer how much federal tax to withhold.

Why It Matters

Wrong settings = owing taxes or getting smaller paychecks. Update it when life changes!

From Gross to Net: Your Paycheck Journey

6 / 20

The 20-30% Gap

The difference between what you earn and what you receive is often 20-30% of your gross income or more.

These deductions aren't just "lost money"—they fund government programs, personal benefits, and your future security.

Understanding this gap is essential for creating realistic budgets based on what you'll actually receive.

The W-4 Form: Taking Control

9 / 20

Why Your W-4 Matters

When you start a new job, the W-4 form determines how much federal tax your employer withholds from each paycheck.

Too little withholding: You might owe taxes and penalties when you file your return.

Too much withholding: Smaller paychecks all year, but a larger refund—essentially an interest-free loan to the government.

🔄 Update your W-4 when: Getting married/divorced, having children, starting a second job, or experiencing major income changes.

Voluntary Deductions = Hidden Value

11 / 20

Benefits Are Part of Your Compensation

Voluntary deductions reduce your paycheck but provide significant value. Understanding these helps you make smart choices about your total compensation package.

  • 401(k) Match: Employer matching is "free money"—a 100% return on your contribution!
  • Health Insurance: Employer-subsidized plans cost less than individual coverage.
  • Pre-Tax Savings: HSA/FSA contributions reduce taxable income while covering expenses.
  • Tax Reduction: 401(k) contributions lower your taxable income today.
  • Life Insurance: Basic coverage often provided free by employers.
  • Disability Insurance: Protects your income if you can't work.

💰 A job with $50K salary + $10K in benefits is worth more than $55K with no benefits!

Common Paycheck Mistakes to Avoid

16 / 20

Don't Make These Errors

Many young workers make costly mistakes when it comes to understanding their paychecks and taxes. Here's what to avoid:

  • Budgeting on Gross: Plan your spending based on net income, not the salary number.
  • Ignoring 401(k) Match: Not contributing enough to get full employer match = leaving free money.
  • Wrong W-4 Settings: Leads to owing taxes or giving the government an interest-free loan.
  • Skipping Benefits Enrollment: Missing open enrollment means waiting a full year for coverage.
  • Not Reviewing Pay Stubs: Errors happen—check your deductions are correct each pay period.
  • Delaying Retirement Savings: Every year you wait costs tens of thousands in future wealth.
