Key Terms

4 / 18
Career
Definition

A long-term professional journey encompassing multiple jobs, involving growth, development, and advancement over time.

Example

Starting as a staff accountant, then senior accountant, manager, and eventually CFO over 20 years

Job
Definition

A specific position or role with defined responsibilities, typically performed in exchange for payment.

Example

Working as a cashier at a retail store or an administrative assistant at a company

Key Terms

5 / 18
Self-Assessment
Definition

The process of evaluating your own interests, skills, values, and personality traits to guide career decisions.

Example

Taking personality tests, listing skills, reflecting on what motivates you

SMART Goals
Definition

Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound objectives for effective planning.

Example

"Complete my CPA certification exam within 18 months of graduation"

Jobs vs. Careers

6 / 18

💼 Job

  • Specific position or role
  • Focused on immediate income
  • May be temporary or short-term
  • Defined by tasks and duties
  • May not align with long-term goals
VS

🚀 Career

  • Long-term professional journey
  • Encompasses multiple positions
  • Involves progression and advancement
  • Aligned with interests and goals
  • Requires ongoing development

Self-Assessment: Know Yourself First

7 / 18

Three Key Areas to Explore

1. Interests: What subjects do you enjoy? What activities make time fly? What problems do you like solving?

2. Skills: What are you naturally good at? What have you learned through education or experience?

3. Values: What matters most to you in work? What kind of environment do you thrive in?

💡 Self-awareness is the foundation of smart career planning. Know yourself before exploring careers.

Questions to Ask Yourself:

  • What do I lose track of time doing?
  • What topics do I enjoy learning about?
  • What would I do even without pay?
  • What kind of impact do I want to make?

Career Exploration Resources

9 / 18

Online Research Tools

O*NET OnLine (onetonline.org): Comprehensive database with skills, education, salary, and outlook for hundreds of occupations.

Bureau of Labor Statistics: Occupational Outlook Handbook with detailed career information and employment projections.

CareerOneStop: Career assessments, training opportunities, and job search resources.

Personal Research Methods

Informational Interviews: Conversations with professionals to learn about their daily work and career path.

Job Shadowing: Observing professionals during a typical workday to experience the work environment.

Professional Associations: Networking opportunities, mentorship, and industry-specific resources.

SMART Career Goals

10 / 18
S

Specific

What exactly do you want to accomplish? Who is involved? Why is this important?

M

Measurable

How will you track progress? What metrics will show you've achieved it?

A

Achievable

Is this realistic given your resources, time, and current situation?

R

Relevant

Does this align with your broader career aspirations and personal values?

T

Time-bound

What is your target date for completion? When will you reach milestones?

SMART Goal in Action

11 / 18

❌ Vague Goal

"I want to work in healthcare."

This goal lacks specificity, measurability, and a timeline. It doesn't provide direction for action.

✓ SMART Goal

"I will complete a Certified Nursing Assistant program and pass the state exam within 12 months while working part-time at a clinic to gain direct patient experience."

Specific: CNA certification • Measurable: Pass state exam • Achievable: Part-time work + study • Relevant: Healthcare goal • Time-bound: 12 months

Real-World Example

13 / 18
👩‍⚕️
Sarah: From Job to Career

Sarah started as a receptionist at a medical clinic right after high school—viewing it as just a job to pay bills.

After observing nurses and medical assistants, she became interested in healthcare as a career. She created a development plan with short, mid, and long-term goals.

Her transformation: complete nursing prerequisites, earn RN license, then specialize in pediatric oncology and eventually become a pediatric nurse practitioner.

Short-Term (1-2 yrs)
Prerequisites + Savings
Gain patient experience
Mid-Term (3-5 yrs)
RN Degree + License
Pediatric nursing position
Long-Term (5+ yrs)
Nurse Practitioner
Children's hospital

Real-World Example

14 / 18
💻
Miguel: Career Exploration Process

Miguel, a high school junior interested in technology, wasn't sure which specific career to pursue.

Self-assessment: Identified strengths in problem-solving, communication, and creativity; interests in coding, design, and helping others use technology.

Research: Used the Occupational Outlook Handbook to compare Software Developer, UX/UI Designer, IT Support Specialist, and Computer Science Teacher.

Informational Interviews: Conversations revealed that UX/UI design best combined his technical and creative interests.

SMART Goals Set
UX Course in 6 months
Portfolio with 3 projects by year end
Career Path Found
UX/UI Designer
Combines tech + creativity

Design-focused programs
