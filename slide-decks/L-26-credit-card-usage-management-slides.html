Key Terms

4 / 20

Annual Percentage Rate (APR)

The yearly interest rate charged on unpaid credit card balances

Example: An 18% APR means you pay $180 per year in interest on every $1,000 of balance

Minimum Payment

The smallest amount you can pay to keep your account in good standing

Example: A $40 minimum on a $2,000 balance means 9+ years to pay off and $2,200+ in interest

Key Terms

5 / 20

Credit Utilization Rate

The percentage of your available credit that you're currently using

Example: Using $500 of a $3,000 limit = 16.7% utilization (good for your credit score)

Grace Period

Time between your billing cycle end and payment due date when you can pay without interest

Example: If you pay your full balance by the due date, you pay $0 in interest

How Credit Cards Work

6 / 20

It's a Short-Term Loan

Unlike debit cards that use money you have, credit cards let you borrow money you'll pay back later.

  • Buy now, pay later (within billing cycle)
  • Pay in full = no interest charge
  • Carry a balance = pay interest
  • Minimum payment keeps account current
  • Building credit history with responsible use

Choosing the Right Credit Card

9 / 20

Match the Card to Your Habits

Not all credit cards are created equal. The best card for you depends on how you spend money.

Card A: Low APR

15% APR, $95 annual fee, 3% groceries
Best if: You might carry a balance occasionally

Card B: Travel Rewards

18% APR, $0 annual fee, 2x travel points
Best if: You travel and pay in full monthly

Card C: Cash Back

22% APR, $0 annual fee, 4% gas
Best if: Long commute, always pay in full

Key Insight: If you carry a balance, APR matters most. If you pay in full monthly, rewards and benefits matter more.

Warning Story: James's Challenges

14 / 20

James's Situation

James received a credit card with a $5,000 limit. Excited, he bought electronics, clothes, and treated friends to dinner. Within three months, he had maxed out his card and started making only minimum payments.

What Went Wrong

  • Spent without a repayment plan
  • Confused credit limit with spending money
  • Made only minimum payments
  • Interest compounded monthly

Two Years Later

  • Still owes $4,200 on original $5,000
  • Already paid $1,500+ in interest
  • Credit score damaged
  • Stressed and limited options
