Financial Aspects of Renting

Renting involves paying a property owner for the right to live in their property for a specific period governed by a lease agreement.

Monthly costs include: Rent payment, renter's insurance ($15-30/mo), utilities, and sometimes parking.

Upfront costs: Security deposit (1-2 months' rent) and first month's rent—typically $3,000-$5,000 total in {{STATE_NAME}}.

💡 Renters are NOT responsible for major repairs, property taxes, or maintenance—the landlord covers these costs.

Financial Aspects of Owning

Owning means purchasing property with a mortgage loan, giving you full rights as long as you maintain payments.

Monthly costs include: Mortgage (principal + interest), property taxes, homeowner's insurance, HOA fees (if applicable), and maintenance.

Upfront costs: Down payment (3-20% of ${{STATE_MEDIAN_HOME_PRICE}}) plus closing costs (2-5%).

💰 Each mortgage payment builds equity—your ownership stake grows over time as you pay down the loan.

Factors in Your Decision

9 / 20

What Should Guide Your Housing Choice?

The rent vs. buy decision is deeply personal and depends on multiple interconnected factors. There's no universally "right" answer—only what's right for your specific situation in {{STATE_NAME}}.

  • Financial Readiness: Savings for down payment, credit score, stable income, emergency fund
  • Time Horizon: How long you plan to stay (generally need 5+ years to make buying worthwhile)
  • Career Stability: Job security, potential relocations, remote work flexibility
  • Local Market: {{STATE_NAME}}'s price-to-rent ratio, appreciation trends, market conditions
  • Lifestyle Preferences: Desire to customize, willingness to handle maintenance
  • Family Plans: Growing family may need more space and stability
