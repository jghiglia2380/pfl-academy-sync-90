Key Terms

5 / 20
Short-term Goal

Financial objective to be achieved within one year

Quick wins that build momentum and develop your goal-setting skills

Examples: Emergency fund, new phone, concert tickets, paying off small debt
Long-term Goal

Financial objective that takes more than one year to achieve

Requires patience and strategic planning but yields greater rewards

Examples: College savings, buying a car, investment portfolio, travel fund

Short-Term vs. Long-Term Goals

7 / 20

Short-Term Goals

  • Achievable within 1 year
  • Require smaller amounts of money
  • Provide quick wins and motivation
  • More concrete and easier to visualize
  • Building blocks toward larger goals
  • Great for developing skills

Long-Term Goals

  • Take more than 1 year to achieve
  • Require substantial resources
  • Teach patience and commitment
  • Yield greater rewards over time
  • Connect to major life milestones
  • Require strategic planning

✓ Time-bound: 8 months

Real-World Example

13 / 20
R

Riley's Success with SMART Goals

Strategic planning leads to achievement

Riley wants to buy an $800 laptop for college in 8 months. Using the SMART framework, Riley creates a specific plan: save $100 each month through automatic transfers to a dedicated savings account and track progress using a savings app.

Riley makes the goal achievable by identifying specific adjustments: reducing eating out by $50 monthly and earning another $50 through a part-time job.

Real-World Example

14 / 20
M

Morgan's Struggles with Vague Goals

Lack of structure leads to frustration

Morgan wants to "save some money for the future." Without specific amounts or deadlines, Morgan doesn't feel any urgency to save consistently. Some months, Morgan puts away $50; other months, nothing at all.

Morgan doesn't track progress or have a clear target to work toward. After a year, Morgan has saved only $275 sporadically and feels discouraged about financial planning.
