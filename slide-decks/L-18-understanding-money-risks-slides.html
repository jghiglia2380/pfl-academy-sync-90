Key Terms

4 / 20
Market Risk
Definition

The possibility of losses due to factors affecting the overall financial markets—recessions, global events, policy changes.

Example

Tech stocks fell 30% during a market downturn—affecting all investors in that sector

Financial Risk
Definition

Risk related to a specific company's ability to manage debt and meet its financial obligations.

Example

A company goes bankrupt and can't repay bondholders—investors lose money

Key Terms

5 / 20
Inflation Risk
Definition

The risk that your money's purchasing power decreases over time as prices rise—the "silent thief."

Example

$10,000 earning 1% while inflation is 3% = losing 2% buying power yearly

Fraud Risk
Definition

The danger of losing money to scams, deceptive practices, or fraudulent investment schemes.

Example

"Guaranteed 20% returns with no risk" = almost always a scam!

What is Financial Risk?

6 / 20

Understanding the Possibility of Loss

In financial terms, risk is the possibility that an investment's actual return will differ from what you expected.

This can mean:

  • • Losing some or all of your money
  • • Not earning as much as anticipated
  • • Your money losing purchasing power
  • • Being deceived by fraudsters

⚠️ It's impossible to eliminate all risk—but you CAN manage it with the right strategies!

4
Main Types of Financial Risk

The Four Risk Types:

  • Market Risk (market-wide factors)
  • Financial Risk (company-specific)
  • Inflation Risk (purchasing power)
  • Fraud Risk (scams and deception)

Financial Risk & Inflation Risk

8 / 20

Financial Risk: Company Troubles

Sam's Story: He purchased $3,000 in corporate bonds from a promising startup. The company struggled with cash flow and couldn't repay bondholders.

Result: Sam lost a significant portion of his investment.

Lesson: Always evaluate a company's financial stability before investing. Strong finances = lower financial risk.

🔍 Check credit ratings and financial statements before buying bonds!

Inflation Risk: The Silent Thief

Michael's Story: He kept $10,000 in a savings account earning 1% interest for 5 years. He ended up with $10,510.

But... Inflation averaged 3% per year. The same goods that cost $10,000 now cost $11,593.

Lesson: Despite earning interest, Michael LOST purchasing power!

📈 Long-term investments must outpace inflation to maintain real value!

Fraud Risk: Protecting Yourself from Scams

Understanding Your Risk Tolerance

10 / 20

How Much Risk Can YOU Handle?

Risk tolerance is your personal comfort level with potential fluctuations in your investments. It's influenced by your goals, timeline, personality, and financial situation.

  • Conservative: Prefers stability. Uncomfortable with significant fluctuations. Prioritizes protecting what you have.
  • Moderate: Can tolerate some ups and downs. Seeks balance between growth and protection.
  • Aggressive: Comfortable with substantial fluctuations. Focuses on maximum growth potential over time.
  • Key factors: Your age, time horizon, financial goals, income stability, and emotional response to losses.

🎯 Knowing your risk tolerance helps you choose investments you can stick with through market ups and downs!

Managing Risk Through Diversification

11 / 20

Don't Put All Your Eggs in One Basket

Diversification means spreading your money across different types of investments to reduce risk.

Different assets respond differently to economic events. When one investment goes down, others might go up—smoothing out your overall returns.

The Power: If all your money is in one stock and it tanks, you could lose everything. But if you're spread across 20 investments, one failure won't devastate you.

🥚 Diversification is one of the few "free lunches" in investing—reduce risk without sacrificing returns!

↓ Risk
More Diversification

Ways to Diversify:

  • Different asset classes (stocks, bonds, cash)
  • Different sectors (tech, healthcare, energy)
  • Different company sizes (large, mid, small)
  • Different regions (US, international)

Asset Allocation: Dividing Your Portfolio

12 / 20

Balancing Risk and Return

Asset allocation is how you divide your investments among different asset categories based on your risk tolerance, time horizon, and goals.

Stocks: Higher risk, higher potential return. Best for long-term growth.

Bonds: Moderate risk, moderate return. Provides income and stability.

Cash/Equivalents: Lower risk, lower return. Safe but doesn't grow much.

⚖️ Your ideal mix depends on when you need the money and how much volatility you can handle!

Real-World Example: The Power of Diversification

14 / 20
😊
Morgan's Diversified Portfolio

Morgan invested the same $20,000 but spread it across an index fund covering 500 companies.

When that same tech company crashed, Morgan's portfolio only dropped about 15% because the loss was just a small part of the total.

Better yet: While tech struggled, Morgan's healthcare and consumer goods stocks actually went UP, partially offsetting the losses. Their portfolio recovered within a year.
