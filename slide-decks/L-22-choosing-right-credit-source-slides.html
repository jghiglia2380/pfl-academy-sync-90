Key Terms

4 / 20

Annual Percentage Rate (APR)

The yearly cost of borrowing money, expressed as a percentage, including interest and fees

Example: A credit card with 18% APR means you'd pay $180 annually on a $1,000 balance

Collateral

An asset that a borrower pledges as security for a loan, which the lender can seize if payments aren't made

Example: Your home serves as collateral for a mortgage; your car for an auto loan

Key Terms (continued)

5 / 20

Installment Credit

A loan repaid in fixed monthly amounts over a set period of time

Example: A 48-month car loan with $350 monthly payments is installment credit

Creditworthiness

A measure of how likely a borrower is to repay debt, based on credit history and financial factors

Example: A credit score of 750 indicates high creditworthiness to lenders

Traditional vs. Alternative Lenders

7 / 20

Traditional Lenders

  • Lower interest rates (typically 4-18% APR)
  • Strict qualification requirements
  • Longer approval processes
  • Established consumer protections
  • Build credit history when payments reported
  • Banks, credit unions, established finance companies

Alternative Lenders

  • Much higher interest rates (often 100%+ APR)
  • Minimal qualification requirements
  • Very fast approval (minutes to hours)
  • Fewer consumer protections
  • Can create dangerous debt cycles
  • Payday lenders, title loans, rent-to-own

Banks vs. Credit Unions

8 / 20

Choosing Between Institutions

Both offer similar products, but their structure and priorities differ significantly:

  • Banks: For-profit, shareholder-owned, wider ATM networks
  • Credit Unions: Non-profit, member-owned cooperatives
  • Credit unions often offer 0.5-2% lower rates
  • Banks may have more branch locations and services
  • Credit unions require membership eligibility

Key Factors for Comparison

9 / 20

What to Evaluate When Choosing Credit

Always compare multiple lenders using these critical factors before borrowing:

APR

The true annual cost including interest and fees - the most important comparison factor

Loan Terms

Length of repayment period affects monthly payment and total interest paid

Fees

Application, origination, late payment, and prepayment penalty fees

Credit Requirements

Minimum credit score and income documentation needed

Collateral

Whether the loan requires security like a home or vehicle

Flexibility

Options for payment timing, amounts, and hardship programs

Why Your Credit Source Matters

The High Cost of Wrong Choices

Choosing the wrong credit source can cost you thousands and trap you in debt cycles:

  • A $1,500 loan at 8% APR costs $1,562 total over 12 months
  • The same loan from a payday lender could cost $3,000+ due to fees and rollovers
  • 12 million Americans use payday loans annually
  • Average payday borrower spends $520/year in fees alone
  • 80% of payday loans are rolled over or followed by another loan within 14 days

Maya Needs $1,500 for Car Repairs

Situation: Maya's car broke down and she needs it for work. She has a credit score of 650 (fair) and needs the money within a week.

Options Available:

• Credit Union Personal Loan: 8% APR, 12 months = $1,562 total

• Credit Card: 18% APR = $1,648 total (if paid in 12 months)

• Payday Loan: 400% APR equivalent = $2,250+ total

Best Choice
Credit union loan saves $688 compared to payday - worth waiting 2-3 days for approval

Key Takeaways

18 / 20

Compare APR: Always look at the Annual Percentage Rate to understand the true cost of borrowing, including all fees

Shop Around: Get quotes from at least three different lenders before committing to any credit product

Avoid Predatory Lenders: Payday loans and title loans should only be a last resort due to extremely high costs

Calculate Total Cost: Look beyond monthly payments to understand the total amount you'll repay over the loan term
