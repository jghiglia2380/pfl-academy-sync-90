Key Terms

4 / 20

Risk

The possibility of loss or gain in any situation where the outcome is uncertain
Example: Investing in stocks carries the risk of losing money but also the opportunity for gains

Risk Management

The process of identifying, assessing, and controlling threats to minimize negative outcomes
Example: Installing smoke detectors and having fire insurance is risk management

Risk Tolerance

A person's willingness and ability to withstand potential losses
Example: Someone with high risk tolerance might invest heavily in stocks; low tolerance prefers savings accounts

Insurance

A contract that provides financial protection against specific losses in exchange for premium payments
Example: Health insurance pays medical bills after you pay the deductible

Key Terms

5 / 20

Premium

The periodic payment required to maintain insurance coverage
Example: Paying $150/month to your health insurance company to stay covered

Deductible

The amount you must pay out-of-pocket before insurance coverage begins
Example: With a $500 deductible, you pay the first $500 of car repairs after an accident

Risk Avoidance

Eliminating the possibility of loss by avoiding the activity entirely
Example: Not driving during a severe ice storm eliminates accident risk

Risk Transfer

Shifting the financial responsibility for loss to another party, typically through insurance
Example: Buying auto insurance transfers financial liability from accidents

Personal & Property Risk

🎯 Personal Risk

  • Unemployment
  • Identity theft

Impact of personal risks materializing

  • Medical expenses
  • Loss of income
  • Unable to support family
  • Emotional distress

🏠 Property Risk

Potential damage, destruction, or theft of possessions

  • Home damage from fire/flood
  • Auto accident damage
  • Theft of belongings
  • Technology failures

⚠️ Consequences

Impact of property risks materializing

  • Repair/replacement costs
  • Temporary housing needed

Four Risk Management Strategies

9 / 20

🚫 Avoidance

Eliminate risk entirely by avoiding the activity

  • Not driving in ice storms
  • Avoiding risky investments
  • Not lending unaffordable money

🛡️ Reduction

Take steps to minimize likelihood or impact

  • Wearing a helmet
  • Installing smoke detectors
  • Diversifying investments

✅ Acceptance

Acknowledge risk and prepare to handle losses

  • Skipping extended warranties
  • Self-insuring minor items
  • Accepting market fluctuations

🔄 Transfer

Shift responsibility to another party

  • Buying health insurance
  • Getting auto insurance
  • Having life insurance

Choosing the Right Strategy

10 / 20

When to Use Each Strategy

Risk Avoidance

Best when potential loss is severe, benefits don't outweigh risks, or no effective mitigation exists. Limitation: may miss opportunities.

Risk Reduction

Best when risk can't be avoided but can be managed, and benefits outweigh the reduced risk. Limitation: doesn't eliminate risk completely.

Risk Acceptance

Best when potential loss is small, or cost of avoiding exceeds potential loss. Limitation: requires reserves to cover losses.

Risk Transfer

Best for significant but unlikely losses when premium costs are reasonable. Limitation: ongoing premium costs and claim complexities.

How Insurance Works

12 / 20

Insurance Is the Most Common Risk Transfer

Insurance works by pooling risk across many individuals. Everyone pays a premium to protect against losses that will only affect a few.

  • Premiums: Periodic payments to maintain coverage
  • Deductibles: What you pay before coverage kicks in
  • Coverage Limits: Maximum payout for a claim
  • Exclusions: Situations not covered

Real-World Example: Riley's Auto Insurance

13 / 20

Making Smart Insurance Decisions

Riley just bought her first car—a used vehicle worth $8,000. She needs to decide what type of insurance coverage to purchase.

Her approach combines multiple strategies:

  • ✓ Comprehensive coverage beyond minimum liability (risk transfer)
  • ✓ $500 deductible to lower premium (balancing acceptance & transfer)
  • ✓ Anti-theft device for premium discount (risk reduction)

✓ Protection from Major Loss

If her car is damaged or stolen, she only pays the $500 deductible rather than the full replacement cost

📊 Manageable Costs

Higher deductible keeps monthly premiums affordable for her budget

✓ Multiple Layers

Combined approach provides protection while controlling costs

Real-World Example: Marcus's Basketball Decision

14 / 20

Managing Health and Activity Risks

Marcus is deciding whether to join his school's basketball team. He loves basketball but is concerned about potential injuries.

His multi-strategy approach:

  • ✓ Physical exam to ensure readiness (risk reduction)
  • ✓ Sports accident insurance through school (risk transfer)
  • ✓ Proper warm-ups and training (risk reduction)
  • ✓ Not playing when injured (selective risk avoidance)

✓ Enjoys the Activity

Marcus can play the sport he loves without excessive worry

✓ Minimized Risk

Multiple strategies reduce injury likelihood and financial impact

🎯 Smart Balance

Neither complete avoidance nor reckless acceptance—informed management
